The uncertainty response

Two kinds of uncertainty change what the gripper does. The big one is whether the ball comes out at all: the gripper sets off only when the forecast's chance of the ball coming out clears a bar. The second is where and when the ball comes out: a vaguer forecast lowers the chance of a pick-up, which raises the bar.

set off if  P(comes out) > 1.5 / (3.5 × c − 0.5 × (1 − c) + 2.5),  c = pick-up rate
pick-up rate: as the forecast goes from sharp to vague
so the bar rises from

The pick-up rate comes from the policy's own simulation runs, and nothing here is fitted to real outcomes. Rewards: picked up +3 plus up to +1 for an early catch, set off and missed −0.5, ball let go −1, wasted trip −1, held back on a blocked ball +0.5.

After post-training: every real pass, by how sure the model was the ball would come out

Before post-training the model never imagines the ball coming out (the same near-0 score for every pass), so that score carries no information and is treated as no forecast: the gripper falls back to the base rate (42% of balls come out), which clears the bar, so it sets off for every ball.
